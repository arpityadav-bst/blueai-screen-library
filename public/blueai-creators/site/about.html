About

The era where followers mattered is ending. Good.

Social algorithms now surface mostly content from accounts you don’t follow. A big following no longer guarantees an ad gets seen — and a 500-follower creator’s post can go viral if it resonates. Brands have noticed, and the money is moving.

The gap we’re walking into

The programmes exist. The paycheck doesn’t.

Target, Little Spoon, SoulCycle and American Eagle already run programmes built on regular customers. Look closer, though:

Payment is gift cards, discounts and free product.

The WSJ profiled a Louisiana mother who earned a $10 Target gift card for her work — and was grinding toward a $15 one. That detail is the entire reason Blue AI pays cash.

Every programme is a silo.

A creator joins Club Target, Spoon Squad and an affiliate programme separately, and climbs each ladder separately. One profile, many brands is the obvious fix.

The audience knows when someone was paid a lot.

When a famous person endorses a product, viewers discount it. When someone who looks like a peer shows the product, it reads as real. That perceived authenticity is the actual thing brands are buying — we just built the market for it.
