Blue AI · information architecture · draft 1

The full site, structured for one job: get on the list

Everything below is structure only — no screens, no visual design. It takes the eight prototype pages that already exist, adds the pages a real site needs (about, FAQ, legal, per-zone how-it-works), and connects them into one navigable site with two audience zones and a single conversion goal.

Scope of this release: waitlist only. Pricing, log-in, the creator app and the brand dashboard are deliberately out — but each has a reserved slot below, so adding them later is an insert, not a restructure.

Section 1

The model in one paragraph

Two audiences that must never be blended, one brand shell that must never fork. So: two parallel zones — a creator zone at the root and a brand zone under /brands — sharing one header component, one footer component, and one set of shared pages (about, FAQ, legal). Each zone is a straight funnel ending in a single list signup. A persistent switch in the header moves between zones; nothing else does, so neither funnel leaks into the other by accident.

Why the creator zone owns the root

Cold start is supply first (spec §6.6) — creators join for free, and brands will not touch an empty marketplace. Creators also arrive cold, in volume, from search and social, so they need the address you can say out loud. Brands mostly arrive from a conversation or a direct link, so /brands as a named destination costs them nothing.

Rejected: a neutral audience-chooser splash at /. It charges 100% of visitors an extra click, converts nobody, and has no SEO surface of its own.

Why both zones end at a list, not a signup

There is nothing to sell yet. The creator terminal is /waitlist (join, then move up by doing work that pre-builds the matching profile). The brand terminal is /brands/reserve (a founding-brand pilot slot, $0 today).

Different forms, different promises, one goal. Every primary CTA on the site points at one of these two pages — if a CTA points anywhere else, it is a bug.

The one principle carried over from the prototype

Give the number before asking for the email. In the eight-page prototype, two pages captured nothing at all — that inversion was the whole point. In this IA the ratio gets stronger, not weaker: 12 of 18 pages capture nothing. See §9.

18 live URLs, of which 4 are legal boilerplate and 8 already exist as prototype screens. Six pages are genuinely new writing, and one — the creator list — is a redesign of something we already have.

One page I deliberately did NOT create: a standalone /trust

Verification, fraud policy, disclosure and safety are the strongest material we have, and the instinct is to give them their own page. But that content is already required on both /how-it-works pages, framed for each audience — a creator needs "when do I get paid and how do you check", a brand needs "why is this invoice billable". A third page would restate both and immediately go stale against them.

So: the argument lives in each zone's how-it-works page; the publishable policy lives once at /legal/verification and both link to it. The spec is explicit that this policy is meant to be published (§6.2) — legal is where a policy is citable, not a marketing page.

Section 3

What connects to what

Solid arrows are the funnel — each page's one primary CTA. Dashed arrows are branches and alternate exits. Support pages sit below their zone: they are reachable from the header on every page in that zone, but they are not steps, and nothing in the funnel points down into them.

Scroll sideways if the diagram is cut off →

The three links not drawn above

1. The shared zone. /about, /faq and the four legal pages are reachable from the footer on every page in both zones. That is 18 × 6 arrows; drawing them would turn the diagram into noise. The rule instead: shared pages are footer-reachable everywhere and are never a primary CTA.

2. The zone switch. One link in the header, both directions, on every page. A brand reading the creator pitch is one click from their own zone and vice versa — a two-sided marketplace has to let a visitor discover they are on the wrong side.

3. Backwards. Every funnel page carries a breadcrumb to the previous step, as the prototype already does. Forward is a button; backward is a text link. Never the same weight.

An IA gap the prototype has today

/earnings is a computed page — it needs a handle to exist. Right now a cold hit on that URL silently shows @sarahmakesdinner, which reads as either a bug or someone else's data. Same for /brands/creators, which needs a product URL and a city.

Both need a real empty state that asks for the input, since these are exactly the URLs that get shared, bookmarked and pasted into decks. Any page that computes from an input needs a way to collect that input on arrival.

Section 6

Replacing the map with a ranked creator list

The PM did not like the map. Before replacing it, it is worth being precise about what job the map was doing, because the replacement has to keep that job or the brand funnel loses its strongest page: supply is the one thing a brand cannot verify for themselves.

What the map did well — must survive
  • Density at a glance: 412 creators within 25 miles
  • Geography — they are near you, spread across real neighbourhoods
  • Filterable by niche and platform, with counters recomputing live
  • Anonymised: no handles, no faces — protects creators, and makes reserving the way to see more
What the map could never do
  • Answer “are these people any good?” — a dot has no stats
  • Show price, so it could not lead naturally into the forecast
  • Survive a screenshot into a deck; a canvas of dots explains nothing out of context
  • Feel like inventory. A marketplace shows you listings

The replacement page, top to bottom

  • Head — “412 creators within 25 miles of Austin”, the product Blue AI read, and the Indicative sample badge kept exactly as-is.
  • The four stat tiles, unchanged — creators · combined monthly views · matching your category · median followers. This is the density proof the map carried; it moves up here and keeps recomputing on filter.
  • Neighbourhood strip — horizontal bars, not a map: Downtown 90 · North Loop 62 · East Side 58 · Mueller 52 · South Lamar 46 · Westlake 44 · St Elmo 40 · Del Valle 20. This is how the geography half of the map's job survives without a canvas.
  • Filters + a sort control — niche and platform chips as before, plus the thing a list can offer that a map cannot: Best match (default) · Most local reach · Most engaged · Lowest cost per 1,000.
  • The list itself — roughly 12 rows of 412, still anonymised.
  • Foot — “See all 412 with handles when you reserve” + the primary CTA onward to the comparison.

Row anatomy

#7   Food & drink creator · North Loop    [IG] [TT]    3,240 followers    4,800 median views    6.1% engagement    ~$58 / post    Strong match

Pseudonymous label carries niche + neighbourhood, which is what a brand actually needs before reserving. No handle, no avatar, no name.

What may and may not appear in a row

Allowed, because it is public and Scout can genuinely read it: follower count, median views, engagement rate, niche, neighbourhood, platform mix — and a cost estimate derived from the published rate card. The spec is explicit that the creator index holds public metrics only, and that reading a public account is real work, not theatre.

Not allowed: reliability score, on-time rate, past-brand history, disclosure-compliance rating. Those are real fields in the segmentation schema, but they can only exist after creators have run campaigns. Putting them in a pre-launch row would be inventing traction, which is the one thing this audience will not forgive. If a trust column is wanted, it reads “available at launch” and stays empty.

The cost of this change, stated plainly

A list is more exposed than a map. Anonymous dots can be illustrative and nobody audits them; a row that says “3,240 followers · 4,800 median views · ~$58/post” invites someone to check. So this swap raises the bar on the creator index — the open blocker in §9 of the spec — rather than lowering it.

That is an argument for hand-seeding Austin sooner, not against the list. But it should be a conscious trade, not a surprise: the list is the better page and the more expensive one.

Navigation model

One header component, two configurations. The zone decides which links render; everything else — logo, switch, CTA slot, mobile behaviour — is identical. Same for the footer, which is byte-identical everywhere.

  • Coach /coach

  • CTA Estimate earnings → /earnings

Two fixes this bakes in

Every header link goes somewhere real. Today three of eight do not — creator-side “Channels” and “Why Blue AI” both point at #/creators, and brand-side “Why Blue AI” points at #/brands. They reload the page top. They are also hide-sm, so under 820px the creator nav collapses to one link and a button. Giving each of them a real destination is most of why the new support pages exist.

The brand zone chip currently reads INFLUENCERS. That names what the visitor is buying, not who they are — and the whole pitch is that they are hiring many small creators rather than an influencer. It should read BRANDS. Flagging rather than assuming, since it is the live site's wording.
