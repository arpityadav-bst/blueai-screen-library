What each page does, and what it captures

Reference for walking through the clickable prototype. Eight pages, two journeys, both ending at a waitlist — because pre-launch there is nothing to sell yet. The two swimlanes show who does what on each page; the data flow diagram shows what we actually store.

The thing to say out loud: two of the eight pages capture nothing at all. #/creators/earnings and #/brands/compare exist purely to give something away before we ask for anything. That inversion — give the number first, ask for the email second — is the whole change from the pages we started with, which opened an eight-field form before showing any value.

What to say

The creator makes four moves across three pages. Everything in the green lane is an agent doing work that a talent scout does by hand today.

The page that matters

#/creators/earnings. It asks for nothing and gives a number. If it isn’t believable in two seconds, no other page gets a chance.

The branch

Under 1,000 followers doesn’t bounce — it routes to Coach and rejoins at the waitlist. Kale and Duel both reject at this point.

Swimlane 2 of 2

Brand → founding-brand reservation

Five pages. The brand is asked for two fields on page one and nothing else until page five.

Scroll sideways to follow the flow →

What to say

Three of the five pages ask for nothing. They exist to show supply, win the argument and let the brand price it — in that order.

Where it can end early

Two exits from #/brands/plan: reserve, or take the forecast by email. Both are wins — the second still leaves us a budget, a city and a brief.

Pre-launch honesty

Nothing is charged and no creator is contacted. The reservation is a pilot slot, which is a far easier yes than an unproven purchase.

Only two stores are written by visitors

D2 creator waitlist and D3 brand reservations. Everything else is either read-only config or derived from public data.

What we don’t hold

No bank details, no private account data, no personal identifiers beyond an email. D1 is public metrics only. Worth saying before it’s asked.

The build question this answers

Process 1 and 4 are the only ones needing real infrastructure — a creator index. Everything else is a form, a model and a table.

The one open dependency, visible here

Processes 1 (resolve a handle) and 4 (query local supply) both read D1, the creator index. Until that store has real Austin data, the earnings estimate and the map are both illustrative. Everything else in this diagram is buildable in a sprint.

So the decision to take to the PM is narrow: buy, scrape, or hand-seed D1 for one metro. That single choice unblocks the two strongest pages in the prototype.
